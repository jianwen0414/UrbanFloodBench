Objective: Build an AI-powered "Digital Twin" of a coupled urban hydraulic system to predict flood water levels autoregressively.The Core Challenge: We are modeling a Heterogeneous Graph where 1D underground pipes interact with 2D surface terrain. The system must simulate the St. Venant Equations (Conservation of Mass & Momentum) using Deep Learning. The primary difficulty is Autoregressive Instability: predicting $t+1$ using predictions from $t$ leads to compounding errors that can "explode" over long forecast horizons.The Winning Strategy: **(v4 UPDATE)** A **Unified Heterogeneous GNN** that explicitly couples 1D pipes and 2D surfaces via physics-informed graph edges.  The original "Multi-Fidelity Ensemble" plan (decoupled experts) was superseded after the Unified Engine proved superior — explicit 1D↔2D coupling captures surcharge/drainage dynamics that decoupled models miss entirely.  We still follow the organizer's "Simple data" insight by keeping features grounded in raw physics variables (depth, capacity, rainfall) rather than complex derived features.The Model PortfolioTier 0 (Baseline): XGBoost Regressor. A tabular approach to establish a robust "Score to Beat."Tier 1 (Twin Engines):Engine A (1D): GCN-GRU. Specialized for high-speed, pressurized pipe flow.Engine B (2D): GraphSAGE-GRU. Specialized for slow, diffusive surface spread.Tier 2 (Unified Engine): HeteroGNN-GRU. An explicitly coupled model with 4 directed edge types (pipe_to, surface_to, surcharges_to, drains_to), depth-based targets, and per-model static normalization. ~1.7M parameters (192 hidden channels, 3 GNN layers, 2 GRU layers).2. COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE & RULESOfficial OverviewGoal: Accurately predict water levels for all 1D and 2D nodes across multiple rainfall events.Context: Urban flooding arises from complex interactions between underground drainage (1D) and surface overland flow (2D).Dataset Source: Two different urban models (Model 1 and Model 2) with distinct terrains and networks. The model must generalize across these different graphs.Timeline & PrizesDeadline: 1 March 2026, 11:59 PM (SGT).Private Leaderboard: Released 6 March 2026.Total Prize Pool: 9000 SGD (1st Place: 3500 SGD).The Critical Metric: Standardized RMSEWe are NOT minimizing standard MSE. The competition uses a variance-weighted metric to prevent models from ignoring "quiet" nodes. This change was implemented mid-competition to replace NSE (Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency) due to low-variance instability.The Formula:$$\text{Score} = \text{Mean}_{\text{models}} \left( \text{Mean}_{\text{events}} \left( \text{Mean}_{\text{node\_types}} \left( \text{Mean}_{\text{nodes}} \left( \frac{\text{RMSE}_{i}}{\sigma_{i}} \right) \right) \right) \right)$$The Trap (Mathematical Pitfall):Node A (Flood Zone): High variance ($\sigma=2.0$). An error of 0.1ft is negligible ($0.1/2.0 = 0.05$).Node B (Dry Zone): Low variance ($\sigma=0.002$). An error of 0.1ft is catastrophic ($0.1/0.002 = 50.0$).Implication: Our Loss Function MUST be weighted by $1/\sigma^2$. If we use vanilla MSE, the model will focus on flood zones and ignore dry zones, destroying our leaderboard score.Submission RequirementsFile Format: Single CSV or Parquet file.Columns (Strict Order): row_id, model_id, event_id, node_type, node_id, water_level.Constraint: You must predict for both urban models, all test events, and all node types.Validation: A script checks for missing IDs. Incomplete submissions are rejected.Test Set Structure (The "Warm Start")Spin-up Period ($t=1 \dots 10$):Provided: Full Ground Truth for all dynamic variables (Water Level, Flow, Velocity, Rain).Action: Use these to initialize the hidden states ($h_t$) of the RNN/GRU.Scoring: These steps are NOT scored.Prediction Period ($t=11 \dots End$):Provided: ONLY Rainfall ($R_t$).Action: You must use your model's own prediction from $t-1$ to generate the input for $t$.Scoring: These steps determine the leaderboard ranking.3. DEEP DIVE: THE PHYSICS OF THE DATASETDomain 1: The 1D Underground (Pipes)Nodes: Manholes, Junctions, Inlets.Edges: Pipes (Channels).Physics: Water flows under pressure or gravity. Flow is fast and directional. Can reverse direction (backwater effect).Critical Physical States:Free Surface Flow: Pipe is partially full. Gravity driven.Surcharge: Pipe is full. Pressure driven. Water level exceeds pipe diameter.Flooding: Hydraulic Head > Surface Elevation. Water exits the manhole.Domain 2: The 2D Surface (Terrain)Nodes: Mesh cells (Triangular/Rectangular grid).Edges: Adjacency between cells.Physics: Water spreads via diffusion and gravity. Flow is slow and diffusive. Accumulates in depressions (sinks).Critical Physical States:Inundation: Depth of water on the ground.Runoff: Rain accumulating and flowing downhill.The Coupling (1D-2D Interaction)Drainage (Inlet Flow): Rain falls on 2D nodes $\to$ flows via gravity $\to$ enters 1D network at Inlets.Surcharge (Flooding): 1D pipe fills up $\to$ Hydraulic Head exceeds Surface Elevation $\to$ Water erupts onto 2D nodes.Note: **(v4 UPDATE)** In the Unified Engine, this coupling is modeled as explicit heterogeneous graph edges (surcharges_to, drains_to) derived from 1d2d_connections.csv. The original "Distance to Nearest Drain" feature approach was retired with the Twin-Engine plan.4. TECHNICAL PROTOCOLS **(v4 UPDATE)**1. Loss Function (The Metric Hack)We use Huber loss (δ=0.3) weighted by inverse variance to match the competition's Standardized RMSE metric. Huber is more robust to outliers than MSE, preventing gradient explosions from extreme flood events. Key parameters: clamp_weights=5.0 (prevents explosion on dry nodes), separate 1D/2D loss normalization.2. Curriculum Learning (Scheduled Sampling + Progressive Push-Forward)To solve Autoregressive Drift, we combine teacher forcing decay with progressive push-forward:Teacher Forcing: Warmup for 3 epochs (TF ratio = 1.0), then linear decay over 30 epochs to 0.0.Push-Forward K: Starts at K=3 (short trajectories), progressively increased to K=30 (full-length rollouts). This prevents early-training instability from long autoregressive chains.Gradient Flow: BPTT through the entire rollout (no detach) — the model learns to self-correct compounding errors.3. Event-Based ValidationNEVER split data randomly. We use multi-event holdout validation across both models.Val: Events 3, 9, 15 (held out from both Model_1 and Model_2, giving 6 total validation sequences).Reason: We must test generalization to new rainfall patterns AND cross-model consistency.4. Push-Forward TrainingWe calculate loss on the entire K-step trajectory. Later steps are naturally weighted by the autoregressive chain — errors compound, so the model is penalized harder for long-horizon drift.
$$ Loss = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k \cdot \text{Huber}(\hat{y}_{t+k}, y_{t+k}) \quad \text{where } w_k \propto 1/\sigma^2 $$
5. Prediction Clamping (Per-Type Physics Bounds)1D Nodes: clamp(-2.0, 30.0) — depth below invert is physically rare; extreme floods cap at 30ft.2D Nodes: clamp(-0.5, 15.0) — surface depth is non-negative (small tolerance for numerical noise); extreme surface flooding caps at 15ft.6. Per-Model Static NormalizationStatic features are z-score normalized per-model to handle the large elevation scale differences between Model_1 (293–360m) and Model_2 (23–55m). Normalization statistics are saved in checkpoints for inference-time consistency.
